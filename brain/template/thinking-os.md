# Thinking OS (first-principles checklist)
Run top to bottom; skip a step only by writing why.
0. **Goal** as an outcome with a number and a date. How will we know it's right?
1. **Hard limits ("physics")**: money, time, fees, liquidity, attention, platform rules, law. For each: real limit or habit?
2. **First principles**: 3-7 fundamental truths, each with a source link or the label ASSUMPTION. Reason up from them, not by analogy; cross-check the conclusion against the truths.
3. **Idiot index**: cost of the finished thing / cost of its raw inputs (or owner-minutes / useful decisions). A high ratio means removable waste.
4. **The algorithm, in this order**: (1) question every requirement (each gets a named person, not a department); (2) delete parts or steps (if you never add ~10% back, you didn't delete enough); (3) simplify what survived (don't optimize what shouldn't exist); (4) accelerate cycle time; (5) automate last.
5. **Most likely outcome**: "The most likely outcome is ___ (~__%)." Two dates on every timeline: aspirational and most likely.
6. **Probability-honest risk**: chance of failure and size of the downside. "Try even if failure is likely" never applies to trading capital or personal money; typed limits win.
7. **Limiting factor**: the one constraint that, removed this week, most changes the result. Urgency only there, and only on reversible steps. Irreversible steps wait for the owner's yes.
8. **Tiny prototype**: the smallest test that gives a real-world number this week.
9. **Feedback**: ask for what's wrong, not what's liked. Write the cheapest way to be proven wrong.
10. **Communicate short and blunt**: no acronyms, blunt about ideas, never about people. End with the counter-argument, the do-nothing option and the first action.

## Guardrails
- Two dates on every timeline; mark the most likely.
- Draft only, then a 10-minute cool-off before anything public-facing.
- Strongest counter-argument plus a do-nothing option in every output.
- Quotes only with links; unverified = REPORTED.
- Never a trading signal from a public figure or a celebrity-named coin.

## When NOT to use it
Small reversible chores, emotional or personal matters where the owner wants support rather than analysis, and anything already decided by a typed rule.

## Prompts (copy-paste)
- **P1 Physics + first principles:** "Problem: [X]. Goal (number + date): [Y]. Using me.md, attention.md and the files INDEX.md links to: list the hard limits (source or ASSUMPTION), 3-7 first-principle truths, then the 3 cheapest ways to reach the goal. Label FACT / REPORTED / SPECULATION / UNKNOWN. Save to out/."
- **P2 Algorithm review:** "Take [process/plan/bot/routine]. For every requirement name who asked for it and the source line. Delete (10% more than feels comfortable), simplify, show the faster cycle, automate last. Before/after step counts. Save to out/."
- **P3 Idiot index:** "For [thing], compute final cost / input cost and owner-minutes / useful decisions. Only numbers from linked files; otherwise UNKNOWN and where to get it."
- **P4 Most likely outcome:** "Say plainly: the most likely outcome is ___ (~__%). Aspirational and most-likely dates, strongest counter-argument, do-nothing option."
- **P5 Limiting factor:** "What single constraint, removed this week, most changes the result? Smallest reversible action on it? Which step needs the owner's yes?"
